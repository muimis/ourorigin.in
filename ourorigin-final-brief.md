# OurOrigin — Brand & Website Direction
### A brief for our brand architect, from the founder and co-founder

No visual direction, sketches, or design attempts are included here. This document is philosophy, business context, requirements, and history — the canvas is yours to design.

---

## Who we are

OurOrigin is a direct-to-consumer premium spice brand, sourced single-origin from the Western Ghats — the Nilgiris, Idukki, Wayanad, and Thiruvananthapuram in Kerala. We are founder-led, launching now. Sales begin this week on the site as it currently stands; brand and identity work proceeds in parallel, on its own timeline, and should not gate revenue. Once your work is ready, it gets integrated as a considered update, not a rebuild.

Why this matters commercially, not just aesthetically: premium spice is a crowded, low-differentiation category almost everywhere it's sold. The only real defensibility we have is that the story is true and the positioning is uncompromising — which means the brand has to be distinctive enough to justify a premium price against commodity spice sold for a fraction of it. A generic-feeling site with elevated copy pasted on top doesn't clear that bar. That's the actual business reason the identity work matters, not just taste.

## The philosophy — our soul, unedited

> "Person to person transfer; the thing invaluable with the money. The product is not the soul. The land is not the soul. The soul is the profound, invisible exchange of dignity and effort from one human being to another. The farmer puts an invaluable amount of ethics and effort into the yield — walking a long distance, leaving what belongs to the animals, leaving what the season hasn't finished. The buyer honors that effort. The money is not buying a product — the money is just the mechanism that completes the circuit of respect."

A harvester walks into the forest, takes only what's ripe, leaves what the animals have claimed and what the season hasn't finished, and carries back only what was agreed. This is true at two scales simultaneously: the specific (one named community, one origin, a real distance actually walked) and the universal (the same origin story behind every culture's relationship to labor, since before money existed). The specific belongs on product pages. The universal can be said once, at the widest possible angle, on the homepage.

## What's already fixed — design around these, not instead of them

- **Vocabulary:** cart → *The Transfer Ledger*. Add to cart → *Honor the Decision*. Confirmation → *Honored ✓*. Checkout → *Complete the Transfer*. These exact terms, consistently, everywhere the action occurs — site, packaging, email alike.
- **Name, two registers:** *The First Hands* — the specific, named harvester community, used at the product and origin level. *The First Hand* — the universal, unplaced, ancient register, used once, at the top of the homepage only.
- **Background colour:** `#0c1509`. Confirm against the live production token before treating as final.
- **Photography — two permitted languages, no others:** *The Artifact* (the product itself, suspended in the void, museum-lit, hyper-real) and *The Gesture* (chiaroscuro hands — no face, no posed portrait, no lifestyle stock photography under any circumstance).
- **Governing principle:** *"When you ask 'why this?', the answer must always point back to the source. If any decision doesn't reflect the dignity of the walk or the legacy of the community, it's the wrong decision."*
- **Three permanent commitments:** the origin remains the protagonist (never stripped of context to be "easier to consume"); no exploitation of vulnerability (no pseudo-charity marketing); connection over commerce (the market is the final inch, not the end of the story).
- **Site logic:** the homepage carries the universal story with ecommerce at equal weight, not subordinate to it. Product pages carry the specific journey — real distance, named community, full sourcing detail. The catalog/index page is deliberately plain and functional, for returning buyers who already know the philosophy and just want to buy.

## What's genuinely open — this is the engagement

- The mark and full visual identity system, from scratch.
- The complete colour system beyond the one confirmed background.
- Typeface selection, display and supporting, and the type scale built from it.
- Motion and interaction principles for the site.
- The full collateral system: packaging, unboxing/insert card, shipping and invoice materials, social templates, email templates.
- A brand-recognition standard — what combination of elements should make this identifiable even with the wordmark removed.

## What's already been tried and set aside

Internal exploration only, not professional design work, offered so no time is spent retreading the same ground:

- Two overlapping circles (vesica piscis) — set aside as the most overused shape in organic and wellness branding.
- A leaf-and-seed form woven together — set aside as a multi-concept mixture rather than one reduced idea.
- A ring thinning to a point at one place — set aside on sight as a generic circular loading-spinner glyph.
- Literal topographic or map imagery — set aside early as cold, and as "selling land, not life."
- Tribal or ethnic illustration style — set aside as an appropriation risk, and inconsistent with the brand's "absolute truth" positioning.

The one lesson worth carrying forward from all of this: an invented, free-floating abstract "concept symbol" is the most saturated territory in the entire craft — nearly everything in that category already exists as a stock icon or emoji. A mark anchored to the brand's actual name, or to one specific and unrepeatable reference, is far less likely to collide with something that already exists. Offered as a principle, not a pre-decided direction.

## What "done" looks like

Someone unfamiliar with the brand should see the mark, hear the one-line philosophy above, and understand the connection immediately — without a paragraph of explanation. If it takes explaining, it isn't reduced enough yet. Separately: three elements — the background colour, the mark, and the chiaroscuro hand photography — should together be recognizable as OurOrigin even with the wordmark removed entirely from the frame.

## Deliverables

- Primary mark (vector), plus a variant confirmed legible at favicon size (16–32px).
- Full colour system, accessibility-checked against the background.
- Typeface selection with a defined type scale.
- Mark and wordmark lockups — horizontal and stacked.
- Motion guidance — restrained, one deliberate moment rather than many.
- Applied collateral system: packaging, insert card, shipping/invoice materials, social and email templates.
