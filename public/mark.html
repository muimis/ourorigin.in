Why the tally mark.
The tally mark is the oldest human counting record. It predates writing, currency, and borders. It belongs to no country, no religion, no culture — only to the universal act of counting by hand. A farmer in Kerala, a herder in Ethiopia, a fisherman in Norway, a grower in Colombia: all have drawn this mark on a wall. It is OurOrigin's because it is everyone's.
Concept A tally mark. The universal human record of counting.
Pre-national. Pre-script. Found in every culture.
Stroke centres x = 15 · 28 · 41 · 56
Steps: 13, 13, 15 — the last gap drifts wider.
A hand counting does not keep perfect time.
Stroke heights 82 · 83 · 81 · 83 px — no two are identical
Weight Verticals 7px · Diagonal 9px
The cross stroke is heavier — it is drawn last,
in one committed arc. It closes the count.
Diagonal Cubic Bézier: (7,84) → C 21 68, 48 30 → (70,12)
Visible arc — a stroke drawn with momentum, not a ruler.
Optical sizes 4 separate drawings: Display / Standard / Small / Micro
At small sizes, curves become noise. The mark adapts:
lines replace Béziers, weight increases proportionally.
Colour rule Mark Gold (#C99A5B) only. One colour. Always.
Never two colours in the mark. Never reversed to Forest on Gold.
5 strokes Farmer · Place · Nature · Product · You
You are the diagonal. You close the count.
Without you, it is 4 strokes — incomplete.
02The Wordmark
Primary — Forest on Bone · 118px · Cormorant Garamond 400 (working proxy)
Reversed — Bone on Forest
On the ‹i› integration. "OurOrigin" contains two lowercase ‹i› characters in "Origin". The dot (tittle) above each ‹i› is replaced by a short diagonal stroke in Mark Gold — the tally's cross stroke at 45°, miniaturised. The wordmark reads as lettering. The tally is inside it, not attached.

On the font. Cormorant Garamond is used here as a working proxy only. Claude Garamond's 16th-century type was drawn with a broad-nibbed quill — the strokes carry the memory of a pen, not a grid. It belongs to no single geographic tradition. It is a placeholder for the concept, not the final asset.

What must happen next. The wordmark must be hand-drawn — commissioned from a lettering artist. No public font belongs to OurOrigin. The ‹i› → tally concept is the brief. The final forms must be owned.
